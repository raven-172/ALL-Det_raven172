# ALLDet: Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL)Detection

---

## Clinical Context and Research Motivation

Automated blood-cell analysis is already established in clinical practice. Digital morphology systems such as **AI100 with Shonit** and **Scopio X100HT** can automatically locate and pre-classify blood cells, including suspected blasts, while newer systems also support **blast estimation in bone marrow aspirates** (U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2022; 2023; 2024).

However, these capabilities are generally delivered through specialised microscopy, imaging hardware, proprietary software, and expert-reviewed laboratory workflows. Cost, infrastructure, and accessibility therefore remain important considerations, particularly in resource-constrained settings (Kratz *et al.*, 2019; World Health Organization, 2024).

> **Research opportunity:** The challenge is no longer simply whether abnormal cells can be detected or counted. Instead, this project investigates whether **computer vision can provide selected morphological screening functions—such as cell localisation, blast detection, counting, and morphological description—using a more accessible and computationally efficient imaging workflow.**

The system is intended as a **screening and decision-support tool**, not as a replacement for definitive ALL diagnosis, which requires morphology to be integrated with immunophenotypic, cytogenetic, and molecular findings (Arber *et al.*, 2017).
